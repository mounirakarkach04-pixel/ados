09 · Clinical translation · PIGA two-gate architecture


From research instrument to evidence-led clinical technology


The five workstreams below are research and regulatory objectives, not capabilities demonstrated by this app. Medical urgency is determined by the patient's condition and a meaningful intervention window; the PIGA action gate controls whether this software is permitted to influence clinical action. An urgent case does not unlock an unvalidated tool.


Clinical action admission: Gate 2 = BLOCK. No patient-specific resistance prediction, treatment-switch recommendation, automated triage, or authorized bedside use. The research app cannot become an approved medical device by checking boxes.




Workstream 1 · Regulatory
Medical-purpose authorization, not a toggle
Needed: a precise intended purpose, EU MDR qualification and Rule 11 risk classification, verification, clinical evaluation, safety, quality management, cybersecurity, and applicable conformity or FDA processes.
  • MDR Class IIa, IIb or III is risk- and purpose-dependent, not invariably Class III.
  • FDA Breakthrough Device designation is not marketing authorization; the US pathway depends on the particular function.
  • Separate medical-purpose product and independently verified permissions are necessary before any authorized clinical deployment.
BfArM qualification & classification ↗ · FDA CDS guidance ↗


Workstream 2 · Patient benefit
OS, PFS, safety and independent comparators
Needed: prespecified disease-specific use case, standard-of-care comparator, clinically meaningful endpoint and statistically sound analysis; evidence requirements must match claim and regulatory pathway. A demonstrated PFS difference is not an OS benefit.
  • Prospective study design, approvals, safety monitoring and sufficient follow-up.
  • External reproducibility, bias controls, subgroup safety and full event adjudication.
  • Never assert a Phase III trial was performed by PIGA.
SERENA-6 clinical example ↗


Workstream 3 · Adaptive resistance
Serial ctDNA signal verification
Needed: validated sampling and assay intervals, limits of detection, paired molecular/radiological outcomes, confounders, uncertainty bounds and time-to-detection analysis.
  • Benchmark sensitivity, specificity, PPV, false-alarm rate, calibration and lead time.
  • Compare any earlier warning to independent, prespecified standard care.
  • No fabricated clock-time certainty, such as “resistance in exactly 21 days”.
Status: research hypothesis, no individual resistance forecasting.


Workstream 4 · Synthetic lethality
Testable biological vulnerabilities
Needed: a mechanistic network hypothesis (for example, context-specific TP53/KRAS pathway dependencies), matched genetic controls, rescue experiments and effects in independent organoid/cell systems.
  • Separate tumour-selective activity from general cytotoxicity.
  • Document off-target effects, normal-cell comparators and independent replication.
  • In-vitro success is not a clinically validated treatment combination.
Status: discovery design only; no new treatment demonstrated.


Workstream 5 · Clinical guidelines
NCCN, ESMO and S3 evidence translation
Needed: independently replicated patient-relevant benefit, implementation safety, external guideline appraisal and updates to the clinical evidence base.
  • A professional society guideline is not automatically a binding law or a mandate to purchase named software.
  • Decisions about standard care depend on context, jurisdiction and clinical judgment.
  • No guideline endorsement, mandatory tumour-board use or malpractice determination is claimed.
Status: no inclusion or endorsement asserted.




External benchmark — SERENA-6 (2025), not a PIGA result
In a specific ER-positive/HER2-negative advanced breast-cancer population with emerging ESR1 mutations, 315 participants were randomized. At the published 2025 interim analysis, median progression-free survival was 16.0 months with the studied early therapy switch versus 9.2 months with the comparator; this is a PFS finding, not proof of longer overall survival, and neither arm used this workbench. Assay monitoring was study-specific: a proposed two-week cadence or precisely timed individual resistance event is not established here.
Source: NEJM / PubMed, SERENA-6 primary report ↗. The purpose is to show what independent clinical evidence can look like, not to transfer efficacy to PIGA.

Primary independent benchmark: early resistance detection
A falsifiable protocol should state tumour subtype, alteration, clinically relevant decision horizon, sampling intervals, assay limitations, prespecified comparator, time-to-event definition, and independent external validation.


Evaluate more than accuracy
Prespecify sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (prevalence-dependent), calibration/Brier score, false alarms per tested patient, time-dependent discrimination, detection lead time, missingness, assay error, subgroup performance and downstream harms.


A7SEM Reverse: compulsory disconfirmation
Before any clinical claim, test spurious signals, data leakage, batch effects, selection bias, lead-time bias, ineffective treatment switches, false positives, population drift and external replication failure. A theoretical network mechanism alone is never clinical evidence.


Fixed clinical admission result: BLOCK. Gate 1 may evaluate research source admissibility. No local checkbox, JSON passport, lab protocol or user-supplied metric can unlock clinical Gate 2. Any later product would require independently documented authorization for a specified indication.
